Star testing · Registration form — design questions

Jae H. Jeong's U.S. Taekwondo Center · Star testing · guidelines v0.88 · October 17, 2026 · One question at a time. Answered questions stay in this file with their answer.

Design only — not a working app. Names, dates and fees are examples. These are option sketches for one decision at a time (phone and PC); the full form mockups come after the questions are answered. The page's look (logo, colors, header) is provisional — it is its own question later. Amber text = proposed, not decided.

The registration form is the page families open from an event's link or QR code (v0.72), with no login (v0.33). Assumed: most families will use a phone, so phone frames come first. Already decided: one link per event, families choose their Dan on the form (v0.72); date of birth and email are required (scoring depends on age; results are emailed); gender dropped; no phone, attendance or assistant counts (status file B.2); registration open / closed follows the event (v0.67).

Question 1 — One candidate per form, or several at once?

Answered 2026-10-17: Option C

One candidate per form; Register another refills email, school and payment; the confirmation lists everyone registered on this visit with the family total. Also answered: date of birth is picked from a calendar (Question 7). Recorded in the rulebook as v0.75.

A family may have more than one person testing for a star (siblings, a parent and child). The old Color Belt form took 1–10 students in one go; the Star prototype took one candidate at a time. The fields drawn are placeholders (Questions 3–8 decide them); so are the deadline line, the fees and the confirmation wording.

Option A — One candidate per form, with “Register another”

What it does

  • The form registers one candidate. After sending, a confirmation shows a Register another candidate button; the next form starts with the email, school and payment choice already filled in, so a second sibling only needs a name, date of birth and Dan.
  • Each candidate is its own registration from the start.

Good

  • Shortest, simplest form on a phone; nothing to expand or remove.
  • Likely matches the later screens (Candidates tab, payments, results emails), which are expected to work one candidate at a time. Fewer ways to go wrong when building.
  • Same as the Star prototype.

Bad

  • A family with two candidates sends the form twice (though the second time is short).
  • No single family total shown.

Option B — Several candidates in one form

What it does

  • One form for several candidates: each has its own block (name, date of birth, school, Dan); finished blocks fold into a one-line summary with Edit and Remove; Add another candidate adds a block. Good

Question 2 — One page, or steps with a check-before-sending page?

Answered 2026-10-17: Option B

Two steps: Details, then Check and send (summary with the event and the age on testing day). Recorded in the rulebook as v0.76. Still to confirm: whether families can change a registration after sending.

Mistakes matter on this form: the email is where results go, and the date of birth sets the scoring scale. Proposed: once sent, families can’t change a registration themselves; staff can (Candidates tab, designed later). Fields drawn are placeholders; the calendar for date of birth (v0.75) is drawn closed, and its icon is chosen with the full mockups. Rule 1.4 asks for a check before anything that can’t be undone; sending a registration is one of those, so options B, C and D all fit the rules, and A is the one that skips the check.

Option A — One page, send straight away

What it does

  • Everything on one page; Register sends it straight away (no check before sending). Problems (an empty field, an email without “@”) show under the field before sending.

Good

  • Fewest taps; nothing to understand.

Bad

  • No moment to look over the answers; a mistyped email or a wrong birth year goes through unnoticed, and results may go to the wrong address.

Question 3 — Who fills in the form, and how is it worded?

Answered 2026-10-17: Option A

One neutral wording for everyone; no parent name on the form. Recorded in the rulebook as v0.77.

Star candidates range from children to adults, so the form may be filled in by a parent for a child or by the candidate themselves. This decides the wording (“Candidate’s full name” or “Your child’s full name”) and whether a parent’s name is asked. Step 1 of the two-step form (v0.76) is drawn; the other fields and their order are placeholders (Question 5). If Question 9 adds an acknowledgment or signature (the old Color Belt form had one, likely signed by a parent), it would collect a parent’s name too, so a parent field can also be settled there.

Option A — Neutral wording for everyoneRecommended

What it does

  • Neutral wording that works for anyone: Candidate’s full name (“The person testing for a star.”), Candidate’s date of birth, and Email for results (“Results are sent here. A parent’s email is fine.”). Nobody is asked who they are.

Good

  • One wording for everyone; nothing extra to answer; shortest form.
  • Matches the dashboard’s own word “candidate”, so staff and families use the same word.

Bad

  • A little formal for a parent (“Candidate’s” rather than “your child’s”).
  • No parent name is collected for children.

Question 4 — What if the same candidate is registered twice?

Answered 2026-10-17: Option D

Accepted like any other registration; both entries flagged “Possible duplicate” for staff on the Candidates tab; the public form never reveals who is registered. Recorded in the rulebook as v0.79.

Make-ups never use the form: staff add them to the next event (v0.78). What’s left is the same person being registered twice for one event (sent twice by mistake, or both parents filling it in). “The same candidate” means the same full name (ignoring capitals and extra spaces) and the same date of birth; email alone can’t be used because siblings share one. The check only catches exact repeats: “Alex” vs “Alexander” or a mistyped birth date won’t match. Privacy: most candidates are minors, and any message on the public form that says a named person is registered tells that to whoever typed the name and birth date (the rate limit and spam check of v0.33 make guessing slow, but don’t remove the issue).

Option A — No check; staff remove duplicates

What it does

Good

  • Nothing to build on the form; never blocks a real registration.

Bad

  • Double entries on the roster and printouts until someone notices; possible confusion over who has paid.
