Jae H. Jeong's U.S. Taekwondo Center · guidelines v0.27 · October 16, 2026

Design only — not a working app. Nothing here is built or deployed. Each screen is shown at PC (1024px), tablet (768px), and phone (375px). Dashed amber notes are review callouts, not part of the design.

1. Set your PIN (first sign-in)

Right after a password sign-in when the account has no PIN yet, before Home appears. Appears once per account — and again after an admin resets the PIN. Sets the 4-digit PIN that unlocks the lock screen.

What it does

  • Why a PIN: the screen locks after the account's idle time (45 minutes; 2 hours for the front-desk account), or on "Lock now". The PIN reopens it in a few seconds, so nobody has to retype a 12+ character password every time they come back from the mat.
  • When it appears: right after signing in with email and password, if that account has no PIN yet. It comes up before Home. Appears once per account — and again after an admin resets the PIN. Otherwise PINs are changed from the account menu (Change PIN).
  • Who it's for: the top of the card shows the signed-in account (initials circle, name, email), the same as the lock screen, so the person can see whose PIN they're setting. If it's the wrong account, "Not you? Sign out".
  • Two steps on the same card: step 1 "Choose a 4-digit PIN" (hint under it: "Not all the same (1111) and not a straight run (1234)."), step 2 "Enter it again to confirm". A small "Step 1 of 2" / "Step 2 of 2" line shows where you are.
  • The PIN belongs to the account. Each personal account has its own PIN. The shared front-desk account ("Staff", shrewsburyustc@gmail.com) has one PIN that all desk staff know: whoever signs that account in first sets it, and it's shared the same way the desk password is.
  • Nobody can see a PIN. It's stored scrambled (a one-way code): the app can check whether a typed PIN is right, but the code can't be turned back into the digits. Not even admins can look it up. An admin can only reset it, which brings this screen back at that account's next sign-in.
  • Easy PINs are refused: all the same digit (0000, 1111…) or straight runs (1234, 4321, 0123…). The dots clear and an amber message says "That PIN is too easy to guess. Choose another." (not drawn).
  • If the two entries don't match, it goes back to step 1 with the dots empty and an amber message: "The PINs didn't match. Choose your PIN again." (shown on the phone).
  • When it's done: Home opens and a "PIN set" toast shows bottom-right for 5 seconds (not drawn). No pop-up.
  • No "Skip" / "Not now" as drawn. An account with no PIN can't use the lock screen: after the account's idle time (45 minutes; 2 hours for the front-desk account) it is signed out instead, and the sign-in screen shows the amber note "Signed out after being idle. Set a PIN to unlock faster." The full email + password is needed again (decided v0.26). See the question on the PC frame.

How staff use it

  • PC: type 4 digits on the keyboard, no clicking. The 4th digit moves on to step 2 by itself; type the same 4 again and the 4th digit saves. Backspace removes the last digit. The PC shows step 1 with 2 of 4 digits typed.
  • Tablet and phone: tap the digits on the keypad; "Del" removes the last digit. The 4th digit moves on / saves. The tablet shows step 2, waiting for the repeat.
  • Changed your mind on step 2: a quiet "Back" text link returns to step 1 to choose a different PIN. It's there on every device (drawn on the tablet). Step 1 has no Back: there is nowhere to go back to before the PIN is set, only "Not you? Sign out".
  • Signed in on the wrong account: "Not you? Sign out" (same link as the lock screen) signs out this device only.
  • Front-desk account: enter the desk PIN everyone already uses. Only the very first person to sign it in actually chooses it.

Rules applied

  • 5 — personal 4-digit unlock PIN; the PIN only reopens your own signed-in screen, it's not a login; idle lock after the account's idle time (45 minutes; 2 hours for the front-desk account); 5 wrong PINs → password; no-PIN accounts are signed out after idle (v0.26).
  • 1.1 — one job (set the PIN); no primary button at all, the 4th digit completes each step. "Back" is a quiet text link.
  • 1.4 — errors say what happened and what to do next. Typing it twice is the confirmation; the success toast ("PIN set") has no Undo.
  • 3.3 — no location switcher, rail or top bar: like the sign-in and lock screens, this shows no school data.
  • 2.1 / 2.5 — keypad keys drawn 36px tall, 8px gaps give each a 44px tap area; "Back" sits in a 44px tap area. "Del" is a word because ti-arrow-left already means Back (2.4).
  • 4 — the dots have a spoken label ("2 of 4 digits entered"); error is text + icon, not color alone; works by keyboard only on PC; visible focus ring around the dots.
  • 7 — error amber with ti-alert-triangle, never red; school blue only on the filled dots, focus ring, links and the initials circle (same as the lock screen); 12px minimum text.
  • 8 — success is a 5-second toast, not a pop-up; the error message never times out; it stays until the next digit is typed.

2. Change PIN

Lets the signed-in person replace their 4-digit unlock PIN. Opened from the account menu: Change PIN.

What it does

  • Three short steps in one dialog: 1. Confirm it's you (your sign-in password), 2. Choose a 4-digit PIN (hint under it: "Not all the same (1111) and not a straight run (1234)."), 3. Enter it again to confirm. "Step 1 of 3" etc. shows where you are.
  • Step 1 asks for the password so that someone who walks up to an unattended, signed-in device can't quietly set a PIN they know and use it later to unlock that screen (section 5). Supabase (the login service) limits repeated wrong passwords (rate limiting), so it can't be guessed by trying over and over.
  • Step 1 has Cancel and Continue. Steps 2 and 3 have no Continue button: the 4th digit moves on by itself, the same way the lock screen unlocks on the 4th digit.
  • Same weak-PIN rule as Set PIN: all-same digits (1111) and straight runs up or down (1234, 4321) are refused at step 2 with an amber "That PIN is too easy to guess. Choose another." and the dots clear so they can pick again.
  • If step 3 doesn't match step 2, it goes back to step 2 with an amber "The PINs didn't match. Choose your PIN again."
  • Wrong password at step 1: the field clears and an amber "That password doesn't match. Try again." stays under it until they type again (shown on the phone).
  • Success: the dialog closes and a toast "PIN changed" shows bottom-right for 5 seconds. The new PIN works on every device right away; the old one stops working.
  • Shared front-desk account: when signed in as Staff, a 12px gray line above the buttons says "This is the shared front-desk PIN. Tell the desk staff the new one." Personal accounts don't see it.
  • No PIN yet (for example after an admin reset): the menu item reads "Set PIN" instead and opens the Set PIN flow (screen 1).

How staff use it

  • Click or tap the initials circle, then Change PIN.
  • PC: type the password, press Enter (or click Continue), then type the new PIN twice on the keyboard. No clicking on the PIN steps.
  • Tablet: same steps; the PIN steps use the on-screen keypad ("Del" removes the last digit).
  • Going back: on steps 2 and 3 a quiet "Back" text link (next to Cancel) returns to the previous step, on every device (drawn on the tablet, step 2). On the phone the header back arrow does the same on steps 2 and 3; only on step 1 does it leave and return to Home.
  • Cancelling: the Cancel button, or Esc on a keyboard, closes it at any step and changes nothing; the old PIN keeps working. There is no close (X) icon: Cancel is the one way out.

Rules applied

  • 5 — personal 4-digit unlock PIN; the PIN is not a login, so changing it is protected by the password.
  • 1.4 — typing it twice is the confirmation; the success toast ("PIN changed") has no Undo. Errors say what happened and what to do.
  • 1.1 — one blue button (Continue) on step 1; steps 2 and 3 have none. "Back" is a quiet text link.
  • 8 — success is a 5-second toast, never a pop-up; errors appear under the field and don't auto-close.
  • 7 — errors amber with ti-alert-triangle, never red; Cancel is a plain outline button.
  • 6 — dialog over the page on PC and tablet; full page with a back arrow (ti-arrow-left) on the phone.
  • 3.6 / 4 — Esc cancels, Enter continues; works by keyboard only on PC.
  • 2.1 / 2.5 — keypad keys drawn 36px with 8px gaps (44px tap areas); "Back" and the phone back arrow have 44px tap areas; full-width button only for the single phone submit.
  • 2.4 — icons from the master map only (ti-key, ti-arrow-left, ti-alert-triangle).

3. Change password

Lets the signed-in person change the password they sign in with. Opened from the account menu: Change password.

What it does

  • One dialog with three fields: Current password, New password (hint under it: "At least 12 characters"), and Confirm new password. Buttons: Cancel and Change password.
  • The app first checks your current password (it signs you in again behind the scenes) before saving the new one, so someone at an unattended signed-in device can't change it. Supabase (the login service) limits repeated wrong tries (rate limiting).
  • Minimum 12 characters, set in Supabase. A shorter new password gets an amber message under the New password field.
  • The fields are checked when Change password is pressed, not while typing. If the two new passwords differ, an amber "The new passwords don't match. Type the same password in both." appears under the confirm field (shown on the phone). It stays until they fix it.
  • Wrong current password: amber "That password doesn't match. Try again." under the Current password field; nothing changes.
  • The new password is stored scrambled (a one-way code), like the PIN; nobody can look it up.
  • Success: the dialog closes and a toast "Password changed" shows bottom-right for 5 seconds. You stay signed in on this device. Changing the password signs out this account's other devices; they need the new password to sign in again. For the shared desk account that means the other desk computers will need the new password.
  • Shared front-desk account: when signed in as Staff, a 12px gray line above the buttons says "This is the shared front-desk password. Tell the desk staff the new one." Personal accounts don't see it.
  • There is no "forgot password" self-service. If someone forgets theirs, an admin resets it (v0.16).

How staff use it

  • Click or tap the initials circle, then Change password.
  • Fill the three fields top to bottom (Tab moves between them on the PC), then Change password or Enter.
  • Cancelling: the Cancel button, or Esc on a keyboard, closes it and changes nothing; the old password keeps working. There is no close (X) icon.
  • Phone: opens as a full page; the back arrow cancels and returns to Home.

Rules applied

  • 5 — personal email + password; no self-service reset, admins reset it (v0.16); password still asked every 7 days.
  • 1.4 — typing it twice is the confirmation; the success toast ("Password changed") has no Undo. Errors say what happened and what to do.
  • 1.1 — one blue button (Change password).
  • 8 — labels above fields; errors under the field; success is a 5-second toast, never a pop-up.
  • 7 — errors amber with ti-alert-triangle, never red.
  • 6 — dialog over the page on PC and tablet; full page with a back arrow on the phone.
  • 3.6 / 4 — Esc cancels, Enter submits; works by keyboard only on PC.
  • 2.1 / 2.5 — fields 32px with a mouse, 36px on touch; the phone back arrow has a 44px tap area; full-width button only for the single phone submit.
  • 2.4 — icons from the master map only (ti-password, ti-arrow-left, ti-alert-triangle).
  • New rules to be recorded in the guidelines when approved: 12-character password minimum; weak-PIN rule (no all-same digits, no straight runs); Change PIN asks for the password first.
